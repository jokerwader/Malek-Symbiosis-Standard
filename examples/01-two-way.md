# 01 — a reversible (two-way) consequence, written short

*Invented scenario. See the [note in the index](README.md#before-you-read).*

---

## The situation

A workshop of five people makes furniture to order. Jobs are tracked on a whiteboard by the saw: one line per job, wiped when it ships. The whiteboard has been the record for years and it works — except that nobody can see it from anywhere but the floor.

The workshop lead wants to try a shared spreadsheet for two weeks. The whiteboard stays up and stays current for the whole trial. At the end of the two weeks the team decides whether to keep the spreadsheet, and **that decision is a separate one.**

### Working out which record to write

Before writing anything, the lead checks the four ways a consequence leaves the conversation (section 1c of the standard):

| The question | The answer here |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Does it touch a third party in a way that cannot be undone? | No. Customers see nothing; jobs ship the same way. |
| Does it repeat without you? | No. It is a two-week trial with an end date. Adopting it as the standing record would be a rule that repeats — and that would be its own assessment. |
| Does it run without you? | No. People type into it by hand. |
| Did anybody ask you to skip the check? | No. |

None of the four. And the consequence is reversible: it can be undone, it costs an afternoon, it stays inside the workshop, and it judges nobody.

So the short record is allowed.

---

## The record

```
Consequence: reversible — the whiteboard stays current in parallel, so dropping the trial costs one afternoon and nothing leaves the workshop. GATE: ALLOWED — checked: all eight — closest to closing: What if it repeats — if the sheet quietly becomes the standing record without the decision at the end of the fortnight, the whiteboard stops being kept and nobody in the workshop chose that.
Flag 1 [after]: nobody has said what happens to the sheet if the trial is dropped, and a sheet left half-updated reads as current to whoever opens it next — the workshop lead, by the last day of the trial, closed when the sheet is either taken over as the record or archived with a line at the top saying it is not current.
Limits of this assessment: you did not ask the two people on the late shift whether they can reach the sheet from the floor, and you assumed these two weeks are ordinary ones with no large delivery in them.
```

---

## What to notice

### Three lines is the whole record

Not a shortened version of a longer one. **This is what the standard means when it says overdoing it is a mistake too.**

A reversible, internal, two-week trial that judges nobody does not need eight paragraphs. Writing eight would teach everybody around you that the check is a formality.

### All eight principles were still checked

"Checked: all eight" is not a formality. The lead went through them and had nothing to write on seven — which is a normal outcome on a small matter.

**The standard lets you skip a line you have nothing to say on. It does not let you skip the looking.** You should be able to say what you were looking for. Here:

| Principle | What was looked for | What was found |
|-----------|--------------------|----------------|
| Who feels it | who loses anything | nobody |
| Can they refuse | whether anybody is unable to refuse | no — any of the five can keep using the whiteboard, and nothing happens to them |
| Who answers for it | who agreed, and when | the lead, out loud, before the trial |
| Is it true | whether anybody is being told something untrue | no |
| Where is the catch | whether it works only if somebody misunderstands it | no |
| Should we | why it is worth doing at all | the whiteboard cannot be read off the floor |
| Do we keep our word | whether it walks away from anything already agreed | no |
| What if it repeats | what happens if it becomes the norm | that is the separate decision at the end of the trial |

### The `closest to closing:` line is compulsory, and it is where the checking is written down

Every ALLOWED carries one: one principle, one sentence, one named party.

**It is not an alarm and it does not change the verdict.** Naming *What if it repeats* here does not make the trial risky.

**Why it exists:** a framework gate line that reads the same on every decision cannot be told apart from a line copied out of the template — not by anybody else, and not by you in six months. You cannot pick the closest without ranking all eight, and that ranking is the work.

**What you may not do is invent a worry.** If the honest answer is that nothing came near, the sentence says so, about a specific principle and a specific party.

### There is no verdict line, and that is correct

In the short record you write the verdict only with DO NOT PROCEED. An open `[after]` flag holds nothing up, so the reading is PROCEED — and writing it out would add nothing.

### The flag is `[after]` because the consequence is reversible

**Nobody chose that.** The standard sets the kind of flag from the class of consequence.

And it is still a real flag: it says what is wrong, who closes it, by when, and what closing it means. Drop any one of those four and it stops being a flag and becomes a remark — and remarks bind nobody.

### There is no `Decision:` line

The short record does not have one. It sits inside the conversation where the decision was just described, so it does not repeat it. In this file, the section above does that work.

### The limits of this assessment are filled in

Two honest gaps, one sentence.

The standard allows `Limits of this assessment: none` on a reversible consequence — and then tells you to look again, because usually something is left. Here something was left.
