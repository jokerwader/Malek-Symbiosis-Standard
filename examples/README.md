# MSS — worked examples

Three records, one for each shape a result can take.

The standard ([STANDARD.md](../standard/STANDARD.md)) tells you what to write down. These files show it written down.

Read them after the standard, or beside it. **Nothing here adds a rule.** If an example and the standard ever disagree, the standard is right and the example is wrong.

Each file has the same three parts: **the situation**, **the record**, and **what to notice in it**.

---

## Before you read

**Every scenario here is invented, end to end.** The companies, the people, the numbers, the dates — none of them exist. They were written to show the method working, and for nothing else.

**These are not legal advice and not ethical advice.** A record that comes out ALLOWED here says nothing about your case. A record that comes out NOT ALLOWED here is not a ruling about anybody's business.

What the examples show you is **the shape of the writing**: which lines are required, which drop out, and how a closed framework gate ends a matter.

---

## The three examples

### [01 — a reversible consequence](01-two-way.md)

**The decision:** a furniture workshop tries a shared spreadsheet instead of the whiteboard, for two weeks.

**What it shows:** the short record. Three lines, and that is the whole thing.

**Why it is short:** the consequence is reversible, it stays inside the company, and it judges nobody. Section 1c of the standard allows the short record only when all of that holds at once.

### [02 — an irreversible consequence, gate ALLOWED](02-one-way.md)

**The decision:** a haulage operator signs a twelve-month fixed-rate contract.

**What it shows:** the full record. The framework gate says ALLOWED, one flag is open, and that flag is a job with a deadline and a named owner.

**The line worth studying:** the record carries the unfavourable description and says who wrote it — because on an irreversible consequence that line is required.

### [03 — the framework gate closes](03-not-allowed.md)

**The decision:** an app switches a paid add-on on by default for every free account.

**What it shows:** a closed gate. The business case is good, the review is good, and **none of it counts.**

**The line worth studying:** the unfavourable description with its author is here too. That line follows the class of consequence, not the verdict — so it appears whichever way the verdict went.

Three different industries, three different kinds of decision: an internal working change, a commitment to somebody outside, and a product change that reaches many people at once.

---

## What to look for across all three

**The record gets longer as the consequence gets heavier, and for no other reason.**
Example 01 is three lines because the matter is reversible, internal, and judges nobody. Example 02 is long because it binds somebody outside for a year. If all your records come out the same length, one of them is wrong.

**Nothing is traded against anything.**
In example 03 the purpose is clear, the control is clean, and the review is the strongest of the three. The gate is closed, so the answer is no. That is the whole reason the framework gate stands before the review and not after it.

**The unfavourable description is on both irreversible records, 02 and 03, with the name of whoever wrote it beside it.**
It is not on 01 — and the reason is not that 01 came out ALLOWED. It is that the consequence there is reversible. Each line under the framework gate carries its own condition, and this one follows the class of consequence rather than the verdict.

**Consequences, in the review, is filled in on both irreversible records.**
On an irreversible consequence it is mandatory and has to name the cost of undoing and who carries that cost. Leave it out and the assessment is not valid — that is review rule 3 in chapter 3 of the standard.

**LIMITS OF THIS ASSESSMENT is filled in on all three.**
It is the part that is easiest to leave empty, and the part you may not leave empty.
