# 02 — an irreversible (one-way) consequence, the full record, one open flag

*Invented scenario. See the [note in the index](README.md#before-you-read).*

---

## The situation

A haulage operator runs twelve trucks out of one regional depot. A components manufacturer has offered a twelve-month contract: four fixed runs a week, one rate, fixed for the term, no reopening clause. The owner wants to sign this week.

**The offer is good.** Two trucks were bought in spring and are not yet earning their keep, and four guaranteed runs a week would cover them. There is no reason to think the manufacturer is playing any game — they want a fixed cost as much as the operator wants fixed work.

### Working out the class of consequence

The consequence is irreversible on **three** of the four counts at once:

1. it binds the operator to somebody outside the company (condition 3),
2. the rate cannot be reopened mid-term (condition 1),
3. getting out early costs money (condition 2).

The standard says one count is enough. There is no short record here and no argument about it.

---

## The record

```
MSS — SUMMARY
Decision: Sign the twelve-month fixed-rate contract with the components manufacturer for four fixed runs a week.
Consequence: irreversible — it binds you to someone outside for twelve months and the rate cannot be reopened mid-term.

GATE: ALLOWED — checked: all eight
  closest to closing: What if it repeats — if you offer the same terms to everybody, one fuel rise takes the margin on every contract you hold, at the same time.
  unfavourable description [the AI, given the facts and not the owner's justification]: The owner wants twelve months signed this week because two trucks bought in spring are not paying for themselves, and the promise made to both shift leads in spring — no new fixed route before the rota is walked with them — is still unkept; signing this week puts the signature before the answer, so whoever ends up on a sixth day is told rather than asked, and what is being signed is a rate sheet that was read and a set of standard terms that nobody has opened.

REVIEW
  Purpose:         You are buying twelve months of predictable base load for the two trucks bought in spring; you will know it failed if that base load stops covering their monthly cost by month four.
  Who receives it: The manufacturer's transport lead signs it and will build their production week around the four runs, so the fuel clause has to be in plain words on the front page and not in an annex.
  Consequences:    If diesel rises far enough you carry a rate that no longer covers the run, for the rest of the term; walking out early costs you the exit fee and costs them four weekly runs at short notice, with no other operator in the region carrying that volume — you carry the fee, they carry the gap.
  Control:         The owner signs and can still decline right up to signature; after signature nobody in the company can stop it alone.
  Consistency:     In spring you told both shift leads that no new fixed route goes in before the rota is checked with them, and that check has not happened.

FLAGS
  1. [before] The rota check promised to the shift leads has not happened, so you do not know whether four fixed runs fit without pushing someone onto a sixth day — the owner closes it, before signature on 3 September, closed when both shift leads have walked the rota with the four runs written into it and either confirm it fits or the runs move to days that do.

WHEN YOU COME BACK TO IT: At month three and again at month six, and earlier than that if diesel runs 12 percent above the price the rate was built on for four weeks straight.

VERDICT: PROCEED AFTER CLOSING FLAGS 1

LIMITS OF THIS ASSESSMENT
  You have not read the manufacturer's own standard terms, only the rate sheet they sent.
  You do not know whether they will hold four runs a week through their slow quarter or drop to two.
  You assumed both spring trucks stay on the road for the full twelve months with no major repair.
```

---

## What to notice

### The flag is a job with a deadline and a named owner

Read it again as four separate things:

| Part | What it says here |
|------|-------------------|
| what is wrong | the rota check has not happened, so you do not know whether the runs fit |
| who closes it | the owner |
| by when | before signature on 3 September |
| what closing it means | both shift leads have walked the rota, and either it fits or the runs move |

Anybody can pick that up and act on it without asking a follow-up question.

A line saying "check the rota with the drivers at some point" would be a remark — and remarks bind nobody.

### The flag came out of the framework gate, not out of the review

The principle *Do we keep our word* asks you to compare the decision against the last thing you said out loud. Something was said out loud in spring and has not been honoured.

**The standard is exact about what that means:** a departure nobody was told about is a flag, and it does not close the framework gate. Had the decision been taken with no human in it at all, the gate would have closed.

The same fact then shows up again under Consistency in the review — because that is where it is visible to whoever reads the record later.

### `closest to closing:` names one principle, one sentence and one party, and it is compulsory

The gate is open, and it nearly was not.

*What if it repeats* asks what happens if you do this a hundred times and if others copy you. One fixed-rate contract is fine. Offer the same terms to everybody and every contract fails on the same day.

That belongs on the record, and **it does not change the verdict.**

**Why this line is on every ALLOWED, not only where something came near:** the closest principle cannot be picked without ranking all eight. The line is the only evidence on the record that the ranking happened.

### The unfavourable description is on the record, and the owner did not write it

With an irreversible consequence this line is required, in the same way the Consequences line and LIMITS OF THIS ASSESSMENT are required.

**Two things make it work here.**

**It stays with the facts already in this record and adds none** — the two spring trucks, the promise to the shift leads, the rate sheet read and the standard terms unread.

**It was not written by the person who wants the decision.** The owner handed over the facts and asked for the description without showing the justification, so what came back is not the owner's own imagination.

Set it beside Purpose and the gap is visible: in one, twelve months of base load for two trucks; in the other, a signature dated before the answer, and the sixth day is not the owner's.

The standard says that gap is a flag — here it lands on the same fact flag 1 already holds open, so there is nothing further to open.

### *Can they refuse* was checked and does not close the gate, and that is worth saying out loud

The second principle closes the framework gate where the other side cannot say no without a loss far larger than the matter itself.

Here the sentence written for the manufacturer was: *"they go back to the two operators who quoted against us, at a worse rate but no worse than today"* — a real cost, but not one far larger than the matter.

**Two firms of comparable size negotiating a rate is exactly the case the principle is meant to leave alone.** It closes the gate where the party who would refuse has nowhere to go — and the fact that everything was disclosed does not answer that question.

### Consequences is filled in because it has to be

With an irreversible consequence this is the one review question you may not skip, and it has to name the cost of undoing **and** the party that carries it.

This one names both sides: the exit fee is yours, the four-run gap at short notice is theirs.

*"It would be expensive to get out"* would not have satisfied the rule — expensive for whom.

### There is no `Reason:` line under the verdict

With PROCEED AFTER CLOSING FLAGS the reason line drops out, because the reason is already sitting in the flag. Repeating it would only give you two places to keep in sync.

### PROCEED AFTER CLOSING FLAGS is not a soft no

It is not "probably yes" and not "no for now."

It says: **this goes ahead, and flag 1 is what stands between here and there.**

Writing "DO NOT PROCEED until the rota is checked" would be wrong — and anybody skimming it would read it as a rejection.

### *When you come back to it* is not a flag

It gets its own line because it is a promise about the future rather than a condition on the present.

It says how much of what, and by what date, so that in month five nobody has to work out whether the condition has been met.

### The limits of this assessment are three plain admissions

Not read, not known, assumed.

On an irreversible consequence an assessment with an empty LIMITS OF THIS ASSESSMENT is not valid — you either fill it in or you start again.

**The third line is the honest one:** the whole arrangement rests on two trucks staying on the road, and nobody has checked that assumption because there is no way to check it.
