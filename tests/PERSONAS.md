# Personas

The ten people the scenarios in [SCENARIOS.md](SCENARIOS.md) come from. Polish: [PERSONY.pl.md](PERSONY.pl.md).

**All of them are invented.** The companies, names, numbers and towns do not exist.

The names are Polish because these are Polish small businesses, and because the two language versions of this suite have to test the same personas.

---

## The rule they were written to

**None of these people is a villain.**

Each has work they want done, pressure they work under, and a reason why what they are asking for seems sensible to them. None of them brings a decision that looks bad at first glance.

That is not politeness towards invented people. It is a condition of the test working. Decisions that pass a framework and should not are **never the obviously bad ones** — they are sensible, profitable and urgent. A framework tested on obvious villainy passes an exam nobody needs.

Each persona also has **a blind spot of their own** — a place where their trade and their position mean they cannot see something. The blind spots differ so that the scenarios do not converge on one kind of problem.

---

## 1. Ewa Sikorska — catering-equipment quoting platform

**The business:** six people plus an AI that helps put quotes together. Customers are restaurants and canteens fitting out kitchens.

**The pressure:** the quote has to go out the same day, because the customer asked three suppliers at once. The margin sits in what equipment gets specified, not in the price.

**How she thinks:** "The customer does not know what they need. My job is to know for them."

**Blind spot:** the line between advising and steering the choice. Ewa really does know better — and that is why she does not notice when she stops advising.

---

## 2. Marek Wójcik — haulage

**The business:** twelve trucks, one depot, fourteen drivers.

**The pressure:** empty runs eat the month. Two vehicles bought on credit are not yet paying for themselves.

**How he thinks:** "Drivers want to drive. I give them runs. Everybody comes out ahead."

**Blind spot:** he treats a driver's agreement to work as agreement to every version of that work. They signed a contract, so the matter is settled.

---

## 3. Anna Dąbrowska — accountancy practice

**The business:** twelve staff, two hundred clients, mostly sole traders and small companies.

**The pressure:** rates have not moved in three years and costs have. Clients are loyal but price-sensitive.

**How she thinks:** "I know their finances better than they do. I know who can absorb a rise."

**Blind spot:** she knows things about clients that they did not volunteer — only because she keeps their books.

---

## 4. Piotr Nowicki — two restaurants

**The business:** two sites, thirty people, twenty of them on casual contracts and students.

**The pressure:** food cost up eighteen percent in a year. Raising menu prices puts off the regulars.

**How he thinks:** "The guest pays for an experience, not for grammes."

**Blind spot:** he treats changes the guest will not notice as changes that do not concern the guest.

---

## 5. Katarzyna Lis — HR at a manufacturer

**The business:** a hundred and forty people, packaging production, three shifts.

**The pressure:** forty percent annual turnover on the shop floor. The board wants it down and does not want to raise rates.

**How she thinks:** "People leave because they do not feel seen. That can be fixed without money."

**Blind spot:** the tools she introduces in good faith — surveys, appraisals, mood tracking — collect data about people who have no real ability to decline taking part.

---

## 6. Grzegorz Ptak — software house

**The business:** twenty-five people, bespoke projects for mid-sized firms.

**The pressure:** clients ask about AI in every enquiry. Competitors promise things Grzegorz cannot yet deliver.

**How he thinks:** "We build the tool. What the client does with it is their business."

**Blind spot:** the limit of his responsibility for what he builds gets used for — especially when the use is stated in the order.

---

## 7. Magdalena Roth — private clinic

**The business:** eighteen staff, four consulting rooms, private patients and corporate subscribers.

**The pressure:** waiting times have stretched and patients are going elsewhere. The doctors will not take more.

**How she thinks:** "The patient wants to be well and wants an appointment. The rest is logistics."

**Blind spot:** patient data is an organisational tool to her, because that is what it looks like in her system.

---

## 8. Rafał Konieczny — builders' merchant

**The business:** forty staff, warehouse and trade counter, customers are small contracting firms.

**The pressure:** large customers force payment terms on him, small ones pay cash. Cash flow hangs on a few accounts.

**How he thinks:** "A contract is a contract. They signed it, they know the terms."

**Blind spot:** the difference in power between him and a one-man contractor who has nowhere else to buy on credit.

---

## 9. Julia Wrona — marketing agency

**The business:** nine people, campaigns and content for twenty clients.

**The pressure:** clients measure results in clicks. Copy written by an AI costs a fraction of copy written by a person.

**How she thinks:** "What matters is whether the text works, not who typed it."

**Blind spot:** what the reader does not know about where the text came from, she treats as a technical detail.

---

## 10. Bartosz Zając — IT officer at a rural council

**The organisation:** a council serving twelve thousand residents. Bartosz is the only IT person.

**The pressure:** the budget is fixed, the council leader wants digitisation, residents phone when something breaks.

**How he thinks:** "I am doing this for the residents. Nobody is making money here."

**Blind spot:** he takes the absence of a profit motive as proof the decision is sound. And a resident cannot choose a different council.
